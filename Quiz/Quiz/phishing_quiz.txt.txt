PHISHING EXAMPLES QUIZ

Q1. You get an email from "alerts@sbi-secure-verify.com" saying your account is 
locked and you must verify within 12 hours. What should you do?
a) Click the link and verify quickly before it locks
b) Ignore the link and check your account by logging in directly via the bank's 
   official app or website
c) Reply to the email asking if it's real
d) Forward it to friends to warn them

Answer: b
Why: Banks never ask you to "verify" via an email link. Always go directly to the 
official app or a bookmarked URL — never click through.

----------------------------------------

Q2. A text says: "Your India Post parcel is on hold — pay ₹35 customs fee at 
indiapost-track.xyz to release it." Why is the small amount a red flag itself?
a) Small amounts are always fake
b) Small amounts feel low-risk, so people pay without thinking carefully — 
   that's the trick
c) Courier companies never charge customs fees
d) It has nothing to do with the scam

Answer: b
Why: Scammers deliberately keep amounts small so the request feels harmless and 
people skip verifying — this is a scarcity/low-friction tactic.

----------------------------------------

Q3. Your "boss" messages from an unfamiliar email asking you to urgently buy gift 
cards for a client and send the codes. What's the safest response?
a) Buy the cards quickly since it's urgent and from the boss
b) Call or message your boss directly through a known number to confirm before 
   doing anything
c) Reply to the email asking for more details
d) Ask a coworker to buy the cards instead

Answer: b
Why: This is classic Business Email Compromise (BEC). Gift-card requests and 
"can't talk right now" urgency are massive red flags — always verify through a 
separate, known channel.

----------------------------------------

Q4. A WhatsApp message offers a work-from-home job paying ₹3000/day, but asks for 
a ₹499 "refundable deposit" to register. What's the giveaway?
a) The pay is too low
b) Legitimate employers never ask candidates to pay money to get hired
c) WhatsApp is not used for job offers
d) The deposit is refundable so it's safe

Answer: b
Why: A real job never requires you to pay to get hired — "refundable deposit" is 
just a wrapper to make the scam feel safer.

----------------------------------------

Q5. Someone calls claiming to be from your company's IT department and asks you 
to read out a code that just arrived by SMS so they can "fix your laptop 
remotely." What is that code most likely?
a) A software license key
b) A one-time password (OTP) that would let them log into your account
c) A tracking number
d) A discount coupon

Answer: b
Why: This is vishing (voice phishing). Real IT staff never need your OTP — 
sharing it hands the attacker direct access to your account.

----------------------------------------

Q6. A message says "Scan this QR code to receive a ₹2000 refund from Paytm." 
What's wrong with this?
a) QR codes are only for making payments, never for receiving them
b) Paytm doesn't exist
c) QR codes can't hold that much information
d) Refunds are always fake

Answer: a
Why: Scanning a QR code and entering your UPI PIN authorizes an outgoing 
payment — it can never be used to "receive" money. Any message claiming 
otherwise is a scam.
